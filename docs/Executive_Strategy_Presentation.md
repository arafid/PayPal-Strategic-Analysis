# PayPal Strategic Analysis - Executive Presentation

This document is a GitHub-friendly version of the final team presentation for BAD 4013: Business Strategy and Policy at the University of Oklahoma.

## 1. Company Overview

PayPal is a global digital-payments platform connecting consumers and merchants across more than 200 markets. The project focused on a gap between PayPal's scale and the performance of several of its core assets.

Four strategic issues framed the analysis:

- slowing growth in the core branded-checkout business
- increasing competitive pressure in digital checkout
- weak product evolution and strategic drift
- reputational risk tied to the Honey browser-extension controversy

## 2. Strategic Problem: Slowing Growth in Core Business

Key observations from the project:

- 2025 net revenue reached approximately $33.2 billion, but annual growth slowed from the prior year
- branded-checkout growth weakened
- Q4 revenue missed analyst expectations
- management acknowledged execution issues in branded checkout
- PayPal changed CEOs in the same earnings cycle

**Takeaway:** The slowdown affected a strategically important core business, making it more than a routine weak quarter.

## 3. Strategic Problem: Market-Share Pressure

PayPal faced increasing competition from mobile wallets, fintech platforms, and alternative checkout options including Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shopify, Affirm, Klarna, Zelle, and Cash App.

The project highlighted the shift from desktop commerce, where PayPal historically held a stronger position, toward mobile and embedded payment experiences.

**Takeaway:** PayPal remained large, but competitors were gaining traction where consumer behavior was moving.

## 4. Strategic Problem: Weak Product Evolution

The analysis argued that PayPal had moved away from a more product-led model and toward financial optimization and acquisition-led expansion.

Examples discussed included:

- Braintree and Venmo as major additions to PayPal's ecosystem
- reduced differentiation after the eBay separation
- Working Capital as a useful product but not a strong platform differentiator
- PYUSD as a product whose strategic role and organic demand remained unclear
- commercial arrangements that reduced PayPal's ability to control parts of the payments experience

## 5. Strategic Problem: Honey and Reputational Risk

The project examined allegations that PayPal's Honey extension replaced creators' affiliate links with its own at checkout, creating legal and partner-trust concerns.

The analysis treated this as strategically relevant because PayPal's broader value proposition depends heavily on trust between consumers, merchants, creators, and payment partners.

## 7. Porter's Five Forces

| Force | Assessment | Main Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Threat of new entrants | Low to moderate | Trust, scale, regulation, and established networks create barriers |
| Threat of substitutes | High | Mobile wallets, bank-linked payments, cards, and fintech alternatives are widely available |
| Buyer power | High | Consumers can often switch between payment options with little friction |
| Supplier power | Low to moderate | PayPal depends on card networks, banks, and technology providers but can diversify relationships |
| Competitive rivalry | High | Many established payment platforms compete for consumers and merchants |

**Takeaway:** Competitive rivalry and buyer power were two of the strongest pressures identified in the project.

## 8. VRIN Analysis

The team evaluated four PayPal capabilities:

| Capability | Valuable | Rare | Difficult to Imitate | Non-substitutable |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Brand & consumer trust | Yes | No | No | No |
| Diversified products | Yes | No | No | No |
| AI-powered fraud protection | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Two-sided buyer/merchant network | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |

The most important insight was that PayPal's fraud-detection technology is not unique by itself. The more defensible asset is the scale and history of transaction data used to train and improve that capability.

## 9. Recommendation 1: Shift Toward Organic Growth

### Problem

- PayPal had relied heavily on mergers and acquisitions to expand capabilities
- acquired products remained relatively fragmented
- integration challenges limited the value of the broader ecosystem
- the Honey acquisition illustrated how acquisitions can also introduce legal and reputational risk

### Recommendation

- redirect more acquisition capital toward internal R&D
- deepen existing capabilities such as fraud detection
- strengthen integration across PayPal's product ecosystem
- build infrastructure needed for long-term product development
- reduce dependence on purchasing external capabilities

**Strategic logic:** PayPal's strongest long-term advantages are more likely to come from internally embedded capabilities tied to its data, network, and infrastructure.

## 10. Recommendation 2: Expand In-Person Transactions

### Market Gap

PayPal had a large consumer base, but its scale in online payments had not translated into comparable dominance in physical point-of-sale transactions.

### Existing Assets

- PayPal POS / iZettle infrastructure
- a large merchant network
- hundreds of millions of active buyer accounts
- BNPL and rewards capabilities
- fraud-detection systems supported by transaction data

### Recommendation

- connect online and in-person payment capabilities into one merchant experience
- earn processing revenue from multiple payment methods, including competing wallets
- use rewards, cash back, and BNPL to increase engagement
- expand transaction data that can reinforce fraud detection

**Strategic logic:** PayPal does not need to beat Apple Pay only as a wallet. It can participate as merchant infrastructure even when the customer selects another payment method.

## 11. Risk: Competitive Entrenchment in POS

Established providers such as Block/Square, Toast, and Clover already have deep merchant relationships and integrated ecosystems that may include payments, inventory, payroll, lending, and analytics.

These ecosystems create switching costs and make merchant adoption of another POS platform difficult.

**Implication:** PayPal has relevant assets for POS expansion, but execution and merchant adoption remain meaningful risks.

## 12. Risk: Technological Disruption

AI-driven commerce could change how customers discover, compare, and purchase products. If AI agents increasingly transact on behalf of consumers, the traditional checkout page may become less important.

**Implication:** PayPal's organic R&D strategy should extend beyond legacy checkout improvements to emerging forms of digital commerce.

## 13. Risk: BNPL Regulatory Fragmentation

BNPL remained an attractive growth area, but the regulatory environment was inconsistent across federal and state jurisdictions.

**Implication:** Compliance complexity could affect margins and make nationwide expansion more difficult.

## 14. Overall Strategic Takeaway

The project's recommendations were not based on the assumption that current market conditions would remain unchanged. Instead, the analysis emphasized adaptability.

The central conclusion was that **internal innovation capacity is the foundation for sustainable competitive advantage**, but PayPal must apply that capacity flexibly as competition, regulation, technology, and consumer behavior evolve.
